--- open: heard alone, at sea, or high up ---

From "100 Ambient Atmospheric Soundtracks: Straylight Drones Collection" by John Bartmann.
CC0 1.0 Universal (https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/) - public domain
dedication, no attribution required. Credited here anyway.

  open-lurking-deep.mp3    "Lurking Deep"
  open-mirrors.mp3         "Mirrors of Faolan"

  https://freemusicarchive.org/music/John_Bartmann/100-ambient-atmospheric-soundtracks-straylight-drones-collection

These two were not picked by title. All 101 tracks in that collection were screened by
measuring short-window RMS: steadiness (coefficient of variation) and the number of hard
energy onsets per minute. A bed wants both low. These scored cv 0.17 / 1 onset and cv 0.19 /
2 onsets, against 0.31-0.99 and up to 37 onsets for the rest of the shortlist. The previous
lofi set measured cv 0.38 with 17 onsets per 90s - that was the beat, and the beat was the
problem.

--- encoding ---

Piano at 64kbps mono 44.1kHz; drones at 40kbps mono 22.05kHz, because a drone has nothing
above 11kHz to lose and MPEG-1 at 44.1k silently rounds 40k up to 67k. Both loudness-matched
to -20 LUFS with ffmpeg loudnorm, so the crossfade between beds does not step in level.

-vn matters: the source files carry an attached 3832x3832 PNG cover image, and without it
ffmpeg copies that album art into every output - roughly 0.7MB per file of a picture nobody
can ever see. That alone was a third of the set's size.

18 minutes of music in 6.8MB.

--- everything else you can hear ---

There are no other audio files, and that is deliberate. The sea, the surf, the wind and the
muffle inside a cave are synthesised at runtime from filtered brown noise in src/audio.ts.
The game already knows the height of the swell passing under the player, how far above the
waterline they are, and how fast they are moving. A recording cannot respond to any of that.
